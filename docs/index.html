For six weeks the NVDA/USDG pools on Robinhood Chain looked like great places to provide liquidity. Then our vault's fee income started falling, and I wanted to know why before we built anything else.

The team and I traced 724,235 swaps back to the contracts and wallets behind them to find out who had been paying our LPs. Most of the profitable volume came from memecoin bots using NVDA as a routing hop, and that flow is down 97% from its peak.

Where the volume came from

Our NVDA vault provides hedged liquidity to the NVDA/USDG 0.05% Uniswap v3 pool, where LPs keep 3.75 bps of every trade after the protocol fee.

Daily volume in the pool rose from about $2M in early August to $62.0M on September 3 and held between $15M and $25M through most of September. From October 3 to 8 it traded $2.5M to $6.2M a day.

Some of that volume moved to cheaper pools. A Uniswap v4 NVDA/USDG pool at 0.01% and a Ramses v3 pool started winning flow in the last week of September, and on October 7 they traded $59.3M and $23.1M against $5.7M in ours. A day later the 0.01% pool was back to $7.0M. Aggregators send price-sensitive flow to the cheapest pool available, so this part we expected. Those spikes were brief. Both pools jumped for a few days in early October, with the 0.01% pool trading $29.0M on October 4 and $59.3M on October 7, and both were back down by October 8, at $7.0M and $2.5M. We didn't move our liquidity to follow them, because we wanted to see volume hold up for weeks, not days, before chasing it.

The rest surprised me. I assumed most of that volume was people trading NVDA, and a large share of it came from memecoin bots. Memecoins launched on one of the chain's launchpads are priced in NVDA, so a lot of their buys and sells cross an NVDA pool on their way to ETH. The wallets we traced behaved like volume bots. One made 59 round trips across 28 memecoins in five and a half hours, selling each position a median 6.8 seconds after buying it.

In the weeks of August 31 and September 7, memecoin routing was the largest single class of flow in the pool at $490k to $567k an hour. By the week of October 5 it was $15k. Arbitrage fell from about $540k to $75k an hour, a smaller drop because arbitrage follows NVDA's price and NVDA kept moving.

The SPY and META vault pools showed the same pattern, and the fall was steeper. Memecoin routing made up 87% to 91% of SPY volume from August 24 to September 14. It fell from $255k an hour in the week of August 31 to under $3k in SPY, and from $69k in the week of September 14 to under $1k in META, about 99% in each, against 97% in NVDA.

These were managed operations. The largest router carried $353k an hour at its peak. When another router began fading in early September, an identical 100-byte contract went live at a new address on September 6 and was carrying $106k an hour the following week.

The bots left when the launchpad emptied. Curve trading peaked at about 57,000 trades an hour in the week of September 7 and is down to about 6,300. New pools paired against NVDA peaked at 3,835 in the week of August 24 and fell below 1,000 by mid-September. The bots paid their own gas and had mostly left before the subsidy changed on September 29.

What each trader was worth

We marked swaps against the Lighter NVDA perp five minutes later to see what each class of flow paid our LPs.

Memecoin-routed flow was 24% of volume and left LPs +3.85 bps per dollar, almost the entire fee, because those bots had no view on where NVDA was going. Aggregator and other flow was 39% of volume and earned LPs +4.16 bps. Arbitrage bots were 37% of volume and cost LPs 4.02 bps per dollar after the fee they paid.

Take the memecoin flow out and LP profit falls by about three quarters, from +1.1 to +0.3 bps per dollar. I don't think anyone providing liquidity on the chain knew how much of their return came from those bots, ourselves included.

Passive stock-token LPing today

For a passive LP in the v3 pool, projected APR on a $10k position went from about +78% in the memecoin era (August 21 to September 19) to about −16% from October 1 to 8. Take the memecoin flow out of that same month and it was about +14%. The pool and the fee stayed the same. The change came from who was trading and from how much volume each dollar of liquidity saw, which fell from 39x a day to about 16x.

The cheaper pools didn't escape this, and one week of October data can't rank them. Results swing from day to day. Most of the 0.01% pool's October return came from two days of unusually high volume, October 4 and 7, and its LPs lost money on October 8. The v3 pool's October result rests on two weekday sell-offs, October 1 and 8. Set each pool's two outlier days aside and both land close to break-even: about +10% a year for the 0.01% pool and +6% for the v3 pool. Ramses lands between −10% and +30% depending on where its dynamic fee sits.

These projections assume a passive ±1% range that never leaves range, before hedging costs. Active management can do better or worse. Our live NVDA vault doesn't sit in the pool every minute. It pulls its liquidity when expected fees don't cover what arbitrage takes, and it sits out the open. After all costs, including hedging and swaps, it was down 0.2% over October 1 to 8 and up 0.2% from its September 22 launch through October 8.

Timing matters too. LPs lose 2.27 bps per dollar in the first half hour after Nasdaq opens and lost about 6 bps per dollar around NVDA's August 26 earnings. Overnight and weekend flow earned them +2.2 and +3.1 bps. A static-fee pool charges the same in every hour.

I want to be clear that stock-token LPing still works on Robinhood Chain. With today's volume and static fees, the returns are small and depend on size and trader mix. The fix is a pool that prices trades by when they happen and what they know.

A pool built for stock tokens

A static fee charges every trade the same, and stock-token trades aren't the same. Uniswap v4 lets a pool attach a hook that sets the fee on each swap. We wrote four, each aimed at a loss we measured above.

Design 1 · no oracle

Opening Bell fee

Charge more in the two half hours that lose money.

Rule
The fee goes from 5 bps to 12 bps for the first half hour after Nasdaq opens and to 10 bps for the last half hour before the close, on NYSE trading days only. The hook works out New York time, daylight saving and exchange holidays on chain, so it needs no oracle and no owner.
Replay
Over August 21 to September 19, LP profit in the v3 pool rises from $49.1k to between $60.2k and $75.9k. The low end assumes every ordinary trader leaves those two windows.
Design 2 · no oracle

Opening auction

Sell the opening jump to arbitrageurs instead of giving it away.

Rule
At 9:30:00 the fee starts at 60 bps and halves every 30 seconds until it reaches 12 bps.
Why
The median NVDA move in the first six minutes after the open was 29 bps, and arbitrageurs race to close that gap in the pool. A falling fee makes each of them wait until it drops just under their edge, so the first one to trade pays close to the full gap.
Replay
Only a rough bound, because our reference price has one-minute resolution. The first 90 seconds after the open carry just 1.6% of the pool's volume, and in the first 30 seconds traders had no edge on average (−1.5 bps), so they were mostly ordinary flow that a 60 bps fee would push elsewhere. The losses at the open are spread across the first 20 minutes, which is what Design 1 already prices. Added to Design 1, the auction moves the replay by between −$1.3k and +$3.8k. We'd test it on a live pool before relying on it.
Design 3 · oracle

Directional fee

Charge the trades that close a gap to the market, and only those.

Rule
A keeper writes a reference price into the hook: Chainlink's 24/5 NVDA price during the week and the perp mark at weekends. A swap that moves the pool toward the reference is closing a gap, which is what arbitrage does, and pays 0.75 times that gap. Every other swap pays the base fee. If the reference goes stale, the hook falls back to Design 1.
Evidence
Swaps that moved the pool toward the reference had a +6.0 bps edge before fees. Swaps that moved it away had −0.9 bps. Direction relative to the market separates the traders who cost LPs money from the ones who don't.
Replay
LP profit over August 21 to September 19 goes from $49.0k to $91.2k, up 86%, with a reference price about a minute old.
Cost
The price has to be fresh, and that has a cost. Chainlink's push feed for NVDA on Robinhood Chain only updates on a 0.5% move, often hours apart, which is too coarse for a fee measured in basis points. Its sub-second Data Streams reports are sold by subscription and have to be fetched by a keeper and written on chain. The gas is small, about $0.002 a write at today's prices, or $3 a day once a minute. Staleness is not: the hook nets +1.42 bps per dollar with a reference about a minute old, +0.78 at two minutes and nothing at five. If the keeper stops, the hook falls back to Design 1.
Design 4 · no oracle

Low-toxicity spread

Make whoever reverses a move pay the spread it opened.

Rule
After a swap moves the price, the pool's quote on that side moves with it and the other side stays where it was, the way a market maker widens after a fill. A trade that reverses the move pays the base fee plus the distance back. The gap halves every few seconds, so the reversal gets cheaper until someone takes it: a Dutch auction the pool runs on its own. The open and close surcharges from Design 1 sit underneath. It adapts the low-toxicity AMM proposed on ethresear.ch to a dynamic fee, since a v4 hook can't reshape liquidity on every swap.
Why
No oracle, no keeper, no subscription, nothing to go stale. Sandwich back legs and fast round trips pay the spread they open, so that flow pays LPs.
Replay
Close to Design 1 on these trades, and below Design 3. With the Opening Bell underneath, the best setting we tried (a 4 bp base, half the gap, a 5-second half-life) netted +0.55 bps per dollar against +0.51 for the Opening Bell alone. A replay can't show how traders adapt to a spread, which is where this design would gain, so it's the one we most want to test live.

For our vault, the question is what a dollar of liquidity would earn. To compare fee designs fairly, we ran them on the same trades: every swap in the v3 pool from August 21 to September 19 with the memecoin bots taken out, marked against the Lighter perp. A static 5 bp fee, the v3 pool's design, netted LPs +0.27 bps per dollar traded. The directional hook at a 2 bp base fee netted +1.42, about five times as much, and at a 1 bp base it still netted +0.87. A static 1 bp fee, the design of the 0.01% pool, lost 2.73 bps per dollar on the same trades.

At the v3 pool's October volume of 15.7x a day per dollar of liquidity, those margins come to about +8% a year for the static fee, +25% for the hook at a 1 bp base and +41% at 2 bps, on a $10k position before hedging costs. The hook's margin comes from charging arbitrage rather than from staying small, so it holds up as more LPs join. These are passive numbers, for an LP that sits in the pool all day. Our vault runs an active strategy on top, and that changes the picture in a useful way.

What changes for an active LP

Our vault doesn't sit in the pool all day. It hedges its NVDA with a short perp and steps out of the pool when the market moves fast, because that is when arbitrage takes the most. To see how that works with each fee design, we replayed a simple version on the same trades: out of the pool in any minute where the perp's volatility over the previous 15 minutes was in the top 10%. That works out to about 31% of the volume, and it decides from past prices only.

On the static pool, stepping out nearly triples the margin, from +0.27 to +0.72 bps per dollar, about +8% to +21% a year at the v3 pool's October volume. With the Opening Bell it reaches +1.03 bps, about +29%. On the directional hook it backfires, from +1.42 down to +0.88, because the hook charges arbitrage the most in exactly the fast minutes the strategy skips. Those become the best-paid minutes in the pool. On a hook pool our strategy would keep its hedge and stay in through the moves. The directional hook with the Opening Bell, held through every minute, came out highest of everything we tested at +2.00 bps, about +57% a year. These are model results on August and September trades before hedging costs; the vault's actual returns are in Figure 7.

A spread without an oracle

Design 3 depends on a price feed that someone has to pay for and keep alive. Design 4 needs nothing, which is why we built it. On these trades it doesn't match Design 3 yet, and Figure 13 shows why. The spread does charge arbitrage more than everyone else, about 5 bps against 3 at a 2 bp base, but the directional fee charges arbitrage 11 bps. The trades that cost LPs most follow a move in the outside market, and a spread built from the pool's own history can't see that move until someone trades on it. At a 2 bp base without the Opening Bell, the spread lost money once ordinary traders who hit a wide spread went to a cheaper pool.

The obvious alternative is to follow the flow to the 0.01% pool. A static 1 bp pool only works while it stays small, because arbitrage trades against the deepest pool. Give it the v3 pool's trader mix, which it would inherit as it grew, and its LPs would have lost about $89k over August 21 to September 19 where ours made $49k. The same flow through the directional hook at a 2 bp base fee made $52k, while quoting ordinary traders less than half our current fee.

A hook only helps if aggregators route to it. Uniswap reviews dynamic-fee hooks before its router sends them flow, and checks for verified source, no upgradeable proxy, no custom hook data and no bypassing of the protocol fee. Our hooks are immutable, read the reference price from storage instead of swap data, and leave the protocol fee alone. The reference implementations and their 32 tests are on GitHub:

  • OpeningBellFeeHook.sol (Designs 1 and 2), about 11k extra gas per swap
  • OracleDirectionalFeeHook.sol (Design 3), about 18k extra gas per swap
  • LowToxicitySpreadHook.sol (Design 4), about 24k extra gas per swap
  • MarketClock.sol, SessionFeeSchedule.sol, NyseCalendar.sol and the tests

They are not audited yet.

Here's the plan. Next week we plan to deploy liquidity into some or all of these hooks on NVDA/USDG, pending Uniswap's routing review, and run them in the wild against the existing NVDA pools. We're starting with NVDA because it's the most traded stock token on Robinhood Chain and the one we have the best data for; other stocks come later. We'll keep our current position until the new pools carry real volume, and publish their live numbers as they trade, the same way we published these.
